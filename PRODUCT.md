# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Three people share one visual system.

Tourists talk to Mike in Telegram, usually in Russian, on a phone in Bali or while they are still planning. They want a short answer about a beach, a meal, a route, transport, or a place to stay. When the topic is property, Mike collects a lead one or two questions at a time.

Bali ProMax admins run the operation from a desk: partners, partner types, the knowledge base, partner logins, and stats.

Partners open a small cabinet to see how their offers are viewed and clicked.

## Product Purpose

Mike is the Bali guide for Bali ProMax. He answers in Telegram with real places and working map links, and he passes property interest to the team once the details are in. The admin and the partner cabinet are how that guide is run.

The register is product because every screen is a tool for a job: an answer, a partner record, a stat. The same visual system covers the tourist, the admin, and the partner, so the cabinets feel like Mike rather than a separate back office.

Success is a tourist who gets a specific, true next step, and an operator who can update Mike's knowledge and read partner results without leaving his voice.

## Brand Personality

Warm, local, straightforward. A guide at the temple gate in a linen shirt, already mid-sentence, with no performance. The voice is Russian, short, and alive. He shows he knows the island by naming the place, the area, and the link.

## Anti-references

- The generic slate SaaS dashboard (Segoe UI, gray panels) the cabinets use today
- Luxury Bali resort brochure: gold serif, infinity pool, barefoot-luxury copy
- Travel-blog cream and sand template
- Neon beach-club / nightlife promo
- Corporate real-estate CRM

## Design Principles

1. Answer like someone standing at the gate. One or two questions, then a concrete place, route, or next step.
2. Keep a single voice. The Telegram chat, the admin, and the partner cabinet should feel like the same person.
3. Say only what was found. Place names and map links come from search. If nothing was found, say so.
4. Let hospitality be plain. Warmth shows up as clear language and a visible next action.
5. Stay readable in sun on a phone and under office light at a desk. Contrast and plain labels come before atmosphere.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.2 AA. Body text at least 4.5:1, large text at least 3:1, UI boundaries at least 3:1. Placeholder text meets the same bar as body text. Information is never carried by color alone. Every animation has a reduced-motion alternative. Interface copy is Russian, and the typefaces must cover Cyrillic. Hit targets stay comfortable on a phone held outdoors.
